**Fold, Return and Closure: a Treatise on the FTM World Model**

Francesco Silvano Schauer

## Abstract

Space is described as a closed, folded three-dimensional manifold, pictured as a funnel. Mass is fold, Δs = GM/(3c²). Slope, clock rate and energy follow from it. Because space has no outside, every motion returns in an inner direction that closes at the closure length Λ. From this follow a threshold a₀ = c²/(2Λ) and a rule of motion, a = a_N/(1 − e^{−√(a_N/a₀)}), which is Newtonian above the threshold and gives flat rotation curves below it, without additional mass. The rotation curves of 141 galaxies (SPARC) read a₀ = 1.11·10⁻¹⁰ m/s², the scale of the early sound wave (DESI) 1.10, the shape of the distances of supernovae and BAO 1.09: the same threshold for part and whole. The breathing after Planck reads 1.065. Dark energy is the restoring of the three loops of the torus. From a₀, the measured densities of the baryons and the radiation and the measured weight of the mode follow H₀ = 67.2 km/s/Mpc and Ω_DE = 0.685. All levels are ordered by three symmetries of the torus shape, mirror, threefold and full turn, and by two ladders. Nucleus, atom, substances, the docking of drugs, stars and galaxies show the same forms, tested on nuclear masses (AME2020), on the periodic table (PubChem) and on the galaxies.

## Preface: Terms Are Tools

This treatise describes the world with a few images: funnel, fold, slope, clock rate, return, loop, closure. These words are tools, not new things. Behind each of them stands a mathematical object that geometry named long ago. The most important one is the **manifold**: a space that looks like ordinary flat space in the small, but may be curved, folded and closed in on itself in the large. The surface of the Earth is a two-dimensional manifold: every piece looks flat like a sheet of paper, while the whole is a sphere without an edge.

**Fold** and **funnel** are, in this model, the same as a curved three-dimensional manifold, only named more vividly. We keep our terms because they help to explain: a funnel can be drawn, a metric cannot. Wherever a term has its own precise meaning in the model, it is explained where it first appears. All symbols are collected in Appendix A.

One rule holds throughout: nothing is explained as an additional substance. Everything observed is a state or a rule of the one medium, space itself.

**The course of the treatise.** Part I starts with the funnel: what the fold is, and how slope, clock rate and energy follow from it. Part II closes space: because it has no outside, every motion returns, and the threshold and the rule follow from that return. Part III shows the symmetries that carry the whole, the mirror, the threefold and the full turn, and the two ladders on which all levels are ordered. Part IV goes through the levels from the particle to the galaxy, Part V through the whole with threshold, breathing and dark energy. Part VI explains how to read measurements without mistaking rules for things, what can be tested and what is set, as in the standard model. Every step builds only on the previous ones.

# Part I: The Funnel

## 1 The Funnel as an Image of the Folded Manifold

We live in three spatial directions. We cannot look at a curvature of this three-dimensional space from outside, because there is no outside from which to look. One helps oneself with a cut: take a flat plane through the middle of a body, say the Sun, and draw how long the paths in this plane really are. Where space is folded, the paths inward are longer than flat geometry would lead us to expect. Drawing this extra length as depth pointing downward produces a **funnel**.

The funnel therefore shows the three-dimensional manifold in two dimensions. Its depth is not a place one could fall into, but a measure: a funnel that sinks deeper represents **more space within space**. Between two circles of equal circumference, the funnel holds a longer path than the flat plane. Space contains more there than its boundary reveals.

On the right, the same two circles with the same circumferences on a surface drawn down into a funnel: the radial path between the circles runs down the funnel wall and is visibly longer. In the centre, the extra length is marked as a vertical arrow labelled **Δs**. A small caption: "The depth is not a place but extra length: more space within space." A third, smaller funnel beside it, drawn deeper, shows: more mass, deeper fold.

## 2 The Fold Δs

The **fold** Δs is the extra length in the centre of a body that folded space contains compared with flat space. It is the measure of mass:

```
Δs = GM/(3c²)
```

**Derivation.** The fold grows with the mass, and it is distributed over the body: for a body of mass M, volume V, surface S and radius R, the extra radial length is Δs = (GM/c²)·V/(S·R), the length GM/c² weighted by the ratio of content to boundary. In the language of general relativity this is the excess radius of a homogeneous sphere. The ratio V/(S·R) is a pure shape number: for the sphere, V = (4/3)πR³ and S = 4πR², so V/(S·R) = 1/3. Hence the 3 in the denominator: it is the spatial dimension d = 3. A homogeneous sphere in d dimensions has Δs = GM/(d·c²).

**Examples.** The Earth carries a fold of Δs = 1.478 mm, the Sun of Δs = 492.2 m, the Milky Way with 6·10¹⁰ solar masses of Δs = 197.4 AU. The fold depends only on the mass, not on the radius: a star that inflates keeps its fold.

## 3 The Slope g

The **slope** is the steepness of the funnel wall at a place, what we feel as weight:

```
g = GM/r² = 3c²·Δs/r²
```

It follows from the fold by inserting GM = 3c²Δs. The product g·r² = 3c²Δs is fixed: the fold sits in the centre, and the slope falls off outward with the square of the distance. If a body expands at fixed mass, its edge flattens while the fold in the centre remains (the balloon effect): a red giant is deeply folded and only weakly sloped at its surface.

## 4 The Clock Rate and Time

The **clock rate** dτ/dt is the ratio in which space unrolls at a place, compared with flat outer space. It follows from the fold, since the clock rate at distance r is √(1 − 2GM/(rc²)), and 2GM/c² = 6Δs:

```
dτ/dt = √(1 − 6Δs/r),        δ = 1 − dτ/dt ≈ 3Δs/r
```

**Time** is the difference in unrolling between folded and flat regions, not friction and not a flow of its own. It arises only where space is folded. Between two places it is δ_AB = dτ/dt|_B − dτ/dt|_A. The small quantity δ is called the **spread**: the distance of the clock rate from its flat value of one.

At the **limit radius** r₀ = 6Δs the clock stands still. For the Earth it lies at 8.87 mm, for the Sun at 2.95 km. A clock on the Earth's surface falls behind flat outer space by 0.022 s per year, on the Sun's surface by 67 s. In the Sun's funnel at the Earth's orbit the figure is 0.31 s. On a neutron star (1.4 M☉, 12 km) the clock rate is 0.81.

**Light in the clock rate.** c is the scale of the unfolded envelope: c² = GM/(3Δs) links fold and slope. Measured at the place, light is always c, because clock and light follow the same rate. Counted from outside it is slower, c_eff = c·√(1 − 6Δs/r), a delay of the unrolling, not friction.

## 5 Energy Is Bound Fold

Mass and energy are the same, and both are fold:

```
E = Mc² = 3c⁴·Δs/G
```

Every energy, whether rest, motion or field, is bound fold. When a body binds to another, it gives up fold.

**Derivation of binding.** On a circular orbit in the funnel, v² = GM/r = 3c²Δs/r, hence δ = 3Δs/r = v²/c²: the spread of an orbit is the square of its speed relative to c. The binding energy of a circular orbit is ½mv², that is ½·δ of the rest energy. **Binding gives up half the spread.**

**Examples.** In the hydrogen atom the electron circulates with v/c = α, the fine-structure constant. The binding is therefore α²/2 of the electron's rest energy, 2.661·10⁻⁵ including the co-motion of the nucleus. The measured 13.598 eV (PubChem) is exactly this fraction. The iron nucleus ⁵⁶Fe carries 0.94 % less mass than its nucleons would carry separately (AME2020).

## 6 Orbits and Light in the Funnel

A planet follows the slope that the Sun's fold produces at its orbital radius:

```
v = c·√(3Δs☉/r),        T² = 4π²·r³/(3c²·Δs☉)
```

With Δs☉ the fold of the Sun this gives for the Earth the orbital speed v = 29.78 km/s and the period T of one year. Every motion in the funnel feels two things: the slower clock rate and the extra length. For light both parts are equal:

```
deflection = 6Δs/b (clock rate) + 6Δs/b (fold) = 12Δs/b        solar limb: 0.8756″ + 0.8756″ = 1.7512″
```

Here b is the distance at which the ray passes the centre. Orbits feel both too: the perihelion of an orbit advances by (12π + 12π − 6π)·Δs/p = 18πΔs/p per revolution, with p the parameter of the orbit, its half width through the focus. For Mercury that is 42.98″ per century. Expressed with the spread δ = 3Δs/p: the orbit misses the full turn by 3δ, and light is deflected by 4δ.

The funnel has fixed **steps**: at r₀ = 6Δs the clock stands still, at 9Δs lies the photon sphere, at 18Δs the innermost stable orbit (Sun 4.43 and 8.86 km). The circles 6Δs, 9Δs, 18Δs are 2d, 3d, 6d with d = 3: the whole numbers of the funnel are multiples of the dimension. Every circle is the zero of a rate in δ = 3Δs/r, and the multiples are the factors in front of δ. Around a body without twist the clock at rest runs, counted from outside, with √(1 − 2δ), and on the circular orbit v² = GM/r = δ·c² exactly. The revolution runs across the depth, without fold distance, and rate and speed add in their squares as for perpendicular quantities: the orbiting clock runs with √(1 − 2δ − v²/c²) = √(1 − 3δ). A co-moving gyroscope turns back against the radius in its own time with Ω = √(GM/r³) and runs ahead against the distant stars by 2π·(1 − √(1 − 3δ)) per revolution, weakly 3πδ. A small deviation of the orbit, u = 1/r, follows in the orbital angle the equation d²δu/dφ² + (1 − 6δ)·δu = 0, for bodies and light: the oscillation of the orbit against its revolution is (Re τ_M)² = (ω_rad/ω_azim)² = 1 − 6δ. At 6Δs the clock at rest stops. At 9Δs the orbiting one stops, there the orbit runs with c for the clock at rest at the place, v_loc/c = √(δ/(1 − 2δ)): the meeting point of the orbit. At 18Δs the radial oscillation stops, the orbit runs there with c/2 for the clock at rest, and inside a perturbed orbit does not stay on the circle. The energy of the orbit in rest energy is the square of the clock at rest divided by the orbiting one, (1 − 2δ)/√(1 − 3δ), smallest at 18Δs and exactly one at 12Δs = 4d: inside, no circular orbit is bound. To first order Re τ_M = 1 − 3δ: the orbit misses the full revolution by 3δ, the rotation of the perihelion. The fold lowers (Re τ_M)² below one, down to zero at the innermost stable orbit, and the pericentres run with the revolution. The rule raises it with 1 + χ above one, up to two in the deep regime, and the pericentres run against the revolution. At the photon sphere (Re τ_M)² = −1: the oscillation becomes growth, a deviation of light grows by the factor e per radian, and counted from outside revolution and decay have the same rate c/b_c with b_c = 9√3·Δs, the edge of the shadow. For large l the ringdown is this circling light, ω = (c/b_c)·((l + ½) − i·(n + ½)) with the overtone n, its revolution c/(2π·b_c) at one solar mass 6.2 kHz.

**The trace of the revolution.** After one revolution, φ → φ + 2π, the equation of the deviation, d²δu/dφ² + (Re τ_M)²·δu = 0, maps the state (δu, δu′) linearly, with determinant 1 and trace 2·cos(2π·Re τ_M). Its eigenvalues are a mirror with the fixed product one and the trace as their sum, λ² − trace·λ + 1 = 0. By its trace the map is of the same three kinds as every symmetry of the torus shape, as rotation, shear and stretching with no, one and two fixed directions of the deviation, like the symmetries with the fixed point inside, one and two fixed points on the boundary. Outside 18Δs the eigenvalues lie on the unit circle, e^{±2πi·Re τ_M}: the deviation turns by 2π·Re τ_M per revolution, and no direction stays fixed, except at integer or half-integer Re τ_M, where every direction returns. At 18Δs they merge into one, and the map is the shear (δu, δu′) → (δu + 2π·δu′, δu′), the same form as the full revolution τ → τ + 1: only the neighbouring circle of equal angular momentum stays fixed, every other deviation grows linearly. Inside they separate as real numbers, e^{±2π·√(6δ − 1)}, with the growing and the shrinking deviation as fixed directions, at the photon sphere with the trace 2·cosh 2π = 535.5. At 24Δs = 8d, Re τ_M = ½: the deviation returns after one revolution with reversed sign, the half turn, and the orbit near the circle closes after two revolutions. Newton and the held body, Re τ_M = 1 and 2, have the identity, for every orbit, not only near the circle. The rule alone lowers the trace from 2 at most to 2·cos(2π√2) = −1.72 and takes no circular orbit around a point mass out of the rotation. Far out, the restoring of the end state lowers Re τ_M again to zero: at the last stable circular orbit around a bound region lies the second shear, with the neighbouring circle of equal angular momentum as fixed direction, and up to R_max the map stretches. Around a galaxy whose mode has been handed over, the sequence runs back, with the identity at R_max/√3 and the half turn at √(7/15)·R_max. Around a mass whose field stays GM/r², the half turn lies at R_max/∛5. The stable circular orbits thus lie between two shears, each preceded by the half turn, the inner one set by the folding, the outer one by the restoring.

**The trace on one straight line.** In the orbital angle an orbit obeys d²u/dφ² + u = Σ F_i(u), with L its angular momentum per mass in its own time. Every term F_i ∝ u^{d_i − 3} carries on the circle the share w_i = F_i/u, Σ w_i = 1, and a deviation oscillates with (Re τ_M)² = 4 − d_eff, d_eff = Σ w_i·d_i. For a slope F_i = g_i/(L²u²), and d_i is its flux dimension: Newton d = 3, the rule 3 − χ, the restoring d = 0 with a negative share. The folding adds the term 3GMu²/c², with d = 5. It is no slope at the place, it depends on the revolution: counted from outside the revolution stays Keplerian, in its own time it is faster, therefore Newton carries only 1 − 3δ, the square of the orbiting clock, and the folding the remainder 3δ. Thus d_eff = 3 + 6δ, the point at the fraction 3δ on the segment from 3 to 5, and (Re τ_M)² = 1 − 6δ. On the straight line (Re τ_M)² = 4 − d_eff lie the three kinds of the trace, below 4 the rotation, at 4 the shear, above it the stretching. The identity lies at 3 and 0, Newton and the held body. The mirror of forces that carries one into the other keeps Re τ_M·Re τ_M′ = 2 fixed, its mirror point is the deep regime at 2. The half turn lies at 15/4. The rule lowers d_eff, folding and restoring raise it, both past 15/4 up to 4. Inside, the shear lies in the middle of the segment, at 18Δs Newton and folding each carry half. At its end, the photon sphere 9Δs, d_eff = 5 and (Re τ_M)² = −1: the orbiting clock stands still, Newton carries nothing, and the folding alone holds the orbit. Light has in the orbital angle only its term and therefore circles only there.

**The generators on the line.** Every symmetry of the torus form has an integer trace. A rotation therefore has the form of such a symmetry only at trace 0 or ±1, at any other trace between −2 and 2 it is no symmetry of the form. On the line, between integer and half-integer Re τ_M, where every direction of the deviation returns, the trace is zero in the middle, at Re τ_M = ¼ and ¾: the map has the form of the mirror τ → −1/τ, every direction returns after two revolutions, and the orbit near the circle closes after four. At one third and two thirds, at ⅙ and ⅓, ⅔ and ⅚, the trace is ±1: the map has the form of the threefold rotation, every direction returns after three revolutions, and the orbit closes after three or six. Between two identities thus lie threefold rotation, mirror and threefold rotation, and with the shear all three generators stand on the line. The fold runs through two such sequences, from Newton to the half turn at 24Δs and from there to the shear at the innermost stable orbit. The rule around a point mass, Re τ_M from 1 to √2, runs through one sequence and does not reach the identity at 3/2: the threefold rotation at a_N = 3.17·a₀, the mirror at 1.12·a₀ and the threefold rotation at 0.23·a₀.

**The winding on the line.** With the energy of the carrier at any speed, W = √(p²c² + m²c⁴) − K_el/r, an orbit around a winding follows in the orbital angle d²u/dφ² + u = K_el·W/(p_φ·c)² + α_φ²·u, with K_el = |Q₁Q₂|/(4πε₀) the coupling of opposite windings, p_φ the angular momentum and α_φ = K_el/(p_φ·c). On the circle α_φ is exactly its speed relative to c, at any speed, like α = v/c in the atom. Like Newton, Coulomb carries the square of the orbiting clock, 1 − α_φ², and the second term the remainder α_φ² with d = 4. It is no slope, it comes from the energy of the carrier, W + K_el·u, which grows inwards. Its share depends only on the angular momentum, not on the depth, so the term is linear in u, and every orbit, not only one near the circle, turns as a rosette by the same angle, (Re τ_M)² = 1 − α_φ². Around the winding the orbiting clock carries no clock rate, only the speed: at equal speed the share is v²/c², a third of the 3δ of the folding, which also carries the clock rate 2δ, and the segment, from 3 to 4, is half as long as the one from 3 to 5. Per revolution the orbit therefore advances weakly by πα_φ², a sixth of the folding. The shear lies at K_el = p_φ·c, with angular momentum ħ at |Q₁Q₂| = q_P², the meeting point of the winding, Zα = 1. Beyond it the map stretches, and every orbit falls into the centre. Thus the orbit around a winding reaches c at its shear, the one around a folding only at the end of its segment.

# Part II: The Closed Space

## 7 No Outside: the Torus

Space has no edge and no outside. It loops back into itself. The simplest form of such a space in three directions is the **torus T³**: take a cube and declare opposite faces to be the same. Whoever walks out on the right comes back in on the left. Every direction therefore has a finite **loop**, a closed path that leads around space. The torus T³ has three independent loops.

Because there is no outside, fold cannot drain away anywhere. When funnels fold extra length into their centres, the closed medium answers with a stretching of the loops:

```
σ = ΣΔs_centres − ΔL_space = 0
```

The medium thus runs a cycle: flat matrix (Δs = 0), vortex (slope g), fold (extra length Δs in the centre), return through the closed whole. Charges, by contrast, cancel on the closed space, ΣQ_el = 0. And the form of the slope is constrained: the torus has Euler characteristic zero, so every slope obeys funnels − filament saddles + wall saddles − voids = 0 (Section 28).

## 8 The Inner Direction and the Return

Because space is closed, every motion returns. This **return** does not run in space, but in an **inner direction** ϖ, orthogonal to space: a half-line from the place, closed at the **closure length** Λ. It is not a spatial direction. One cannot travel in it. It is the path on which the state of a place finds its way back to itself.

The return obeys a single equation, a random walk with loss, for the share u of the state that is at depth ϖ at time t:

```
∂_t u + (Γ + θ)·u = D·∂_ϖ²u,     Γ = a_N/c,     D = GM/c
```

Here a_N = GM/r² is the Newtonian slope at the place, Γ the loss from the clock-rate gradient, θ the loss from the expansion of the region, and D the spreading in the inner direction.

**Derivation of the return fraction.** Stationary and without expansion, for the profile u = G(ϖ) the equation D·G″ = Γ·G remains, with G(0) = 1 (G here this profile, not the gravitational constant). The solution falls off exponentially, and at the return depth ϖ_R

```
q = G(ϖ_R) = e^{−ϖ_R·√(Γ/D)} = e^{−s},     s² = Γ·τ_D,     τ_D = ϖ_R²/D
```

q is the **return fraction**: the share of the state that comes back to the place.

**The state space.** A point of the state space is a complete state of a system, not a place and not a particle. Every independent direction of circulation is a degree of freedom, and n circulations span a torus Tⁿ. Every force acts in it in one of three roles: **tangential** (it conserves energy and entropy and produces circulations and phases, like the electromagnetic one), **transverse** (it produces entropy and pulls onto the torus, like heat and radiation) or **parametric** (it slowly shifts the torus itself). In one equation: ż = 𝓛(z)∇E + 𝓜(z)∇S + ε·F_par, with 𝓛 = −𝓛ᵀ and 𝓛∇S = 0 (tangential), 𝓜 = 𝓜ᵀ ≥ 0 and 𝓜∇E = 0 (transverse). Gravity is parametric: it is not a direction of circulation, but fixes which orbits exist and which degrees of freedom are independent. It acts through two channels, the pre-tension (it changes the stiffness and with it which circulations exist) and the clock rate (it shifts all frequencies alike). A slow change keeps the action E/ω fixed, the torus moves to the neighbouring fibre of equal action. Every inner coupling term appears twice in the energy balance with opposite signs, it only shifts energy. Only gravity and externally driven parameters can supply it. A tangential coupling alone can never lock in. Locking needs loss.

**Derivation of the threshold.** An exactly open direction would have an infinite mean return time. The inner direction is closed at Λ. By Kac's theorem the mean return time to the place is then the path there and back, τ_D = 2Λ/c, the same for every mass. With Γ = a_N/c we get s² = a_N·τ_D/c, and with the **threshold**

```
a₀ = c/τ_D = c²/(2Λ)
```

it follows that s² = a_N/a₀. With D = GM/c the depth of the return is ϖ_R = √(D·τ_D) = √(GM/a₀) = r*: the inner direction reaches as deep as the threshold reaches far in space, and s = r*/r. Kac's theorem holds for every closed state space: for every partition into regions, Σᵢ 1/⟨T_{Aᵢ}⟩ = 1, the mean return time into a region being the total measure divided by its measure. Closure thus distributes a fixed budget of recurrence. An open space sets it to zero. For the mean return times of a closed, stationary system into its states, counted from every moment in them, Σ 1/⟨T⟩ = 1. If a part never returns, the sum stays below one: a direct test of closedness. If the region expands, the loss θ from the expansion is added. Whether the rule acts on a perturbation is then decided by the sum s² = a_N/a₀ + θ̃·τ_D, not by a_N/a₀ alone, with θ̃ the expansion rate of the region, the loss θ of the return equation. The model works with a₀ = 1.06·10⁻¹⁰ m/s². This belongs to Λ = 13.7 Gpc and τ_D = 89.6 billion years. τ_D is longer than the age of the universe: the return is not a process along the orbit, but a stationary loop at a fixed place. Its depth follows from τ_D = ϖ_R²/D with D = GM/c: ϖ_R² = 2GMΛ/c² = r₀·Λ, and that is r*² (Section 10). The return depth is the threshold radius.

## 9 The Rule

Counted at the place, all returns add up: the state comes back with the fraction q, of that again q, and so on. The renewal u(0) = a_N + u(ϖ_R), each return an event in one sense of circulation, gives the **rule**:

```
a = ν·a_N,     ν = Σ qⁿ = 1/(1 − q) = 1 + n_B(s),     n_B = 1/(e^s − 1),     q = e^{−√(a_N/a₀)}
```

ν is called the **enhancement**. It has the form of one plus a Bose occupation. What is counted are returns, not particles. In general a feedback loop of gain q sums all returns to Σqⁿ = 1/(1 − q): q < 0 damps, 0 < q < 1 enhances, q ≥ 1 runs away. The sign belongs to the whole closure, not to the single link.

**The two limits.** If the slope is large compared with the threshold (a_N ≫ a₀), s is large, q → 0, ν → 1: Newton holds exactly. If it is small (a_N ≪ a₀), ν ≈ 1/s, so

```
a → √(a_N·a₀) = √(GM·a₀)/r,     v² = a·r = √(GM·a₀),     v⁴ = GM·a₀
```

The orbital speed becomes independent of the radius: this is the **flat rotation curve** of galaxies, and v⁴ ∝ M is the Tully-Fisher relation. What the observer reads as dark matter is this changed rule of motion, not additional mass. The rule has exactly one branch: d ln a/d ln a_N = 1 − χ/2 lies strictly between ½ and 1, without jump and without hysteresis, with the switch χ = s/(e^s − 1) (Section 15), and d ln a/d ln a₀ = χ/2. In the deep regime the acceleration of the orbit (tangential) is the mean of slope (parametric) and threshold (transversal), a = √(a_N·a₀), and v_flat is at every place the mean of the Newtonian speed, √(GM/r), and the speed of the threshold, √(a₀·r), both equal at r*. The deep regime is scale-invariant (r → λr, t → λt, a → a/λ). From this follows v⁴ ∝ M.

The transition lies at the **threshold radius**, where a_N = a₀:

```
r* = √(GM/a₀)
```

There ν = 1.582. **Example:** the Milky Way as a point mass of 6·10¹⁰ solar masses has r* = 8.88 kpc. The rule gives v = 249 km/s at 5 kpc, 214 at r*, 196 at 15 kpc, 178 at 50 kpc, against the deep value of 170.4 km/s. The Sun has its threshold far outside, at r* = 7480 AU. The planetary system lies deep in the Newtonian regime.

**The balance.** The rule is a balance like that between emission and absorption of light: emission ∝ 1 + n_B, absorption ∝ n_B, and their ratio is q. Newton is the spontaneous part, the return the stimulated one. Decomposed, ν = ½ + ½·coth(s/2): a fixed half and the fluctuating part of an oscillator.

## 11 The Three Generators

## 13 Meeting Points and Spread

The fixed points of the mirror are the **meeting points**: the places where motion reaches c. There, centre, mirror and envelope coincide. At the Planck point this happens completely (3Δs = λ̄ = ℓ_P), and the funnel is degenerate. At the other end of the ladder the whole lies almost the same way, r₀/Λ = 6/(1 + Q), r*/Λ = √(6/(1 + Q)), with Q the weight of the mode of the whole (Section 24).

The **spread** of every level is the speed of its motion relative to c, the distance from the meeting point:

- Around the quantum length it is linear in the speed, a phase, an angle: for the atom α = v/c, and likewise for the fold in 3Δs : λ̄ : λ̄/α_G with α_G = v/c, because at λ̄/α_G the quantum length closes after one revolution around its own fold.
- Around the threshold it is quadratic, a magnitude, a clock rate: for the funnel δ(r*) = 3Δs/r* = (v_flat/c)², the ratio of the radii r₀/r* = 2δ(r*), like δ = v²/c². Linear or quadratic depends on the centre of the ladder, not on winding or fold.

Coupling constants are thus not arbitrary numbers, but distances from the meeting point of their level.

**Which direction carries.** Windings cancel (ΣQ_el = 0), folds add. The winding therefore carries until the fold of N nucleons reaches the winding, N^{2/3}·α_G = α (binding per nucleon, up to factors of order one, independent of the holder):

```
M = m_p·(α/α_G)^{3/2} = 2.30·10²⁷ kg,     depth there δ = α²·m_e/m_p = 2.9·10⁻⁸
```

The same limit read in the depth: below it the depth of a body is smaller than the binding of its envelopes in rest energy of the nucleons, the atoms hold it (Earth δ = 7.0·10⁻¹⁰). Above it the fold holds it with its heat, and the envelopes dissolve (Sun δ = 2.1·10⁻⁶). The fold reaches the meeting point of its holder at N^{2/3}·α_G = 1, on the mass scale of the stars M_* = m_Pl³/m_p². The two limits stand in the ratio α^{3/2} = 6.2·10⁻⁴. The return carries where the envelope reaches the threshold, R ≥ r*, below the surface density a₀/(πG) = 242 M☉/pc²: in the galaxies. In the whole all three directions meet, r₀, r* and Λ lie almost together.

So on every level a different direction of the same funnel carries: particle and atom the winding, planet to star the fold, the compact funnels the fold at the meeting point of their holder, held by the blocking of the half turn (the white dwarf by the windings of the electrons, the neutron star by those of the nucleons), the galaxy the return, the whole all three.

## 14 The Ladder of Lengths

Five lengths place every mass: the quantum length λ̄ (at the place), the Planck length ℓ_P, the fold 3Δs, the threshold radius r* (in space) and the **length of action** ℓ₀ = 2Λ (in the inner direction). They form a **ladder**. Where two lengths become equal lies a distinguished mass. Their positions hang on a single number, the ratio of the Planck length to the length of action:

```
ε_L = ℓ_P/ℓ₀ = 1.9·10⁻⁶²

M_min = ε_L·m_Pl = ħ/(c²τ_D)                 λ̄ = ℓ₀
M_c   = ε_L^{1/3}·m_Pl = 5.8·10⁻²⁹ kg        r* = λ̄
m_Pl                                          3Δs = λ̄ = ℓ_P
M_U   = m_Pl/ε_L = c³τ_D/G = 1.1·10⁵⁴ kg      r* = 3Δs = ℓ₀
```

These are not bodies, but mass scales at which two lengths become equal. M_c divides the ladder at one third: there λ̄ = r* = ℓ_P^{2/3}·ℓ₀^{1/3}. Both ends are set by the return time τ_D, the lower one through ħ, the upper one through G. Without closure the ladder would have no ends. The mirror M ↔ m_Pl²/M exchanges λ̄ and 3Δs and maps M_min onto M_U. The map r → r*²/r is a mirror of the ladder as well: it maps the limit radius r₀ onto the closure length Λ, space onto the inner direction. In units of the upper rung M_U the Tully-Fisher relation is simply M = M_U·(v_flat/c)⁴, and the fold of this mass is Δs(M_U) = ⅔·Λ. As a length the vacuum part lies in the middle of the ladder, at the threshold of the Planck mass: r*(m_Pl) = √(ℓ_P·ℓ₀) = 0.117 mm, and ρ_s·c² = (π³/30)·ħc/r*(m_Pl)⁴. The one number of the ladder counts the baryons of the cell: with Λ = (1 + Q)·ΣΔs_b, ε_L = 3m_Pl/(2(1 + Q)·N_b·m_p), so with 1 + Q = 2π, ε_L·N_b = (3/4π)·m_Pl/m_p. The cell holds N_b = 1.6·10⁸⁰ baryons, counted from ρ_b·L³ alike.

**By directions.** The three basic lengths of every funnel belong to the three directions of the torus: λ̄ at the place tangential, 3Δs the depth parametric, ℓ₀ the inner direction transversal. Their means taken in pairs join two directions: ℓ_P = √(λ̄·3Δs), r* = √(3Δs·ℓ₀) and r̃ = √(λ̄·ℓ₀). Here r̃² = ħ·τ_D/M: this is how far the quantum motion of the body, D = ħ/(2M), reaches into the inner direction in one return time, r̃ = √(2D·τ_D), as its fold with D = GM/c reaches r* = √(D·τ_D). The mirror M ↔ m_Pl²/M exchanges λ̄ and 3Δs and with them r̃ and r*, r*/r̃ = M/m_Pl, and both cross at m_Pl at the length of the vacuum part. To M_c belongs the mirrored m_Pl²/M_c = 8.1·10¹² kg, where 3Δs = r̃ at the length ℓ_P^{2/3}·ℓ₀^{1/3}. Below M_c the threshold lies within the quantum length: for the electron λ̄_e = 510·r*, its field outside the quantum length lies entirely below a₀. Above it the threshold lies outside, for the proton r* = 154·λ̄_p. Below m_Pl²/M_c the body's own quantum motion reaches further than its depth, r̃ > 3Δs. So the ladder has five meeting masses, ε_L^k·m_Pl with k = 1, ⅓, 0, −⅓, −1, pairwise mirror images about m_Pl. At the ends five of the six lines meet: at M_min λ̄ = r̃ = ℓ₀ and r* = ℓ_P, at M_U 3Δs = r* = ℓ₀ and r̃ = ℓ_P. At the bottom the quantum length fills the closure and the threshold is one Planck length. At the top the fold fills the closure and the body's own quantum motion reaches only one Planck length in a return time: there lies the whole, its baryons carry (3/4π)·M_U, with r̃ = 2.05·ℓ_P. In the plane of mass and hull, particles and atoms lie below the line r̃ and Earth and galaxy above it. A body held by atoms, R = a_B·N^{1/3}, crosses r̃ at 2.0·10⁻⁸ kg and r* at 3.1·10⁻⁸ kg, almost at the crossing point of both: lighter grains lie like the atoms below r̃ and above r*, heavier ones like Earth and star above r̃ and below r*.

The ratio of two basic lengths is a **coupling** on the edge between their directions: α_G = 3Δs/λ̄ = (M/m_Pl)² between parametric and tangential, 3Δs/ℓ₀ = (v_flat/c)⁴ between parametric and transversal and α_I = λ̄/ℓ₀ = kT_I/(Mc²) between tangential and transversal, the period of the inner direction in rest energy. The triangle closes, α_G·α_I = (v_flat/c)⁴: Tully-Fisher is the product of the two couplings that carry ħ, and ħ drops out. Each coupling becomes one at a rung, α_G at m_Pl, α_I at M_min, Tully-Fisher at M_U, and at M_c α_G and α_I are equal. The mirror inverts α_G and exchanges the other two. The winding has the same edges to the place, α = r_e/λ̄_e and α_I, its third edge stays empty, the winding has no threshold. On every edge lies a phenomenon: between parametric and tangential the fold at the Planck point, between parametric and transversal the rule of galaxies, between tangential and transversal entanglement, the phase of the winding at a shared node of the inner direction.

**One formula for the ladder.** Every length of the ladder is λ̄^p·(3Δs)^q·ℓ₀^r with p + q + r = 1, a point in the triangle of the three directions. A body has no further lengths: from M, ħ, G, c and a₀ only two pure numbers can be formed, M/m_Pl and ε_L. Each of its lengths is therefore a point times a number of the form, the horizon 2·3Δs, the thermal length of the fold 8π·3Δs and c times its lifetime 5120π·α_G·3Δs. Every further length needs content, a second mass, a charge, a rotation or a holder. Equivalently ℓ = ℓ_P·(M/m_Pl)^k·ε_L^{−r} with k = q − p, parametric against tangential, and r, the transversal share. The corners are the basic lengths, λ̄ (−1, 0), 3Δs (1, 0) and ℓ₀ (0, 1), the midpoints of the edges ℓ_P (0, 0), r* (½, ½) and r̃ (−½, ½), the centroid is ℓ_P^{2/3}·ℓ₀^{1/3} (0, ⅓). Measuring ln M from m_Pl and ln ℓ from ℓ_P in units of ln(1/ε_L), x and y, every length is the line y = r + k·x, and two lengths meet at x = (r₂ − r₁)/(k₁ − k₂). In the plane x, y the ladder is a rectangle from M_min to M_U and from ℓ_P to ℓ₀: r* and r̃ are its diagonals and cross in the middle, at the length of the vacuum part, λ̄ and 3Δs run as a V from the Planck point in the middle of the lower edge into the upper corners. The rule lies on the diagonal r*: from the mirror point of the loops, r*/2π, to the seam, 5.8·r*, it is 0.025 of the height wide. The electron lies at x = −0.36, left of M_c, the proton at −0.31, Earth and Sun at 0.53 and 0.62, the Milky Way at 0.79, a cluster of 10¹⁵ M☉ at 0.86 and the whole at 0.99. The meeting masses are x = ±1, where λ̄ and 3Δs reach the corner ℓ₀, ±⅓, where they reach the centroid, which is why M_c divides the ladder at one third, and 0 at m_Pl, where they reach each other. The mirror M ↔ m_Pl²/M is k → −k: every pair (k, r) and (−k, r) has a fixed product, λ̄·3Δs = ℓ_P² and likewise r*·r̃ = ℓ_P·ℓ₀. Threshold and quantum return of every body are mirror partners about the length of the vacuum part (0, ½). The mirrors of lengths are point reflections: about r* it maps 3Δs onto ℓ₀, about λ̄ the depth onto λ̄/α_G (−3, 0). Every coupling is an arrow between two points, (M/m_Pl)^{Δk}·ε_L^{−Δr}: α_G (2, 0), Tully-Fisher (1, −1) and α_I (−1, −1), and the arrows of the triangle close. ## 17 Particle, Coupling, Charge

An elementary particle is an oscillating state of the medium. Next to it, space remains practically flat. Gravity and electromagnetism have the same form, a **length of action divided by the quantum length**:

```
α_G = 3Δs_p/λ̄_p = Gm_p²/(ħc) = 5.906·10⁻³⁹          α = r_e/λ̄_e = 1/137.036
```

Two ways of imprinting space at a node must be distinguished. The **fold** is a magnitude: it changes the clock rate and has one sense of circulation, that of loss. The **winding** is a phase: it does not change the clock rate, is lossless and counts in both senses of circulation. Mass is fold, **charge is winding**: the phase of a node has a whole-number winding n around its circulation, the charge is Q_el = n·e with e the elementary charge, and the sign is the handedness. On the torus, n is a topological number (the first Chern number). It changes only when the amplitude passes through zero. This is why Coulomb's law remains 1/r² in every field, and why there is no electromagnetic threshold effect: the argument of the rule of return is the clock-rate gradient, never an electric force. In the state space the Lorentz force acts tangentially: on a moving charge its magnetic part does no work, it conserves energy and entropy and produces circulations and phases. Joule heat J·E is the channel into the transverse role. By Onsager and Casimir the transport splits into a symmetric part (conductivity, heat conduction, Seebeck, Peltier: transverse) and an antisymmetric one (Hall, Nernst: tangential). The magnetic field decides which share circulates and which is lost.

**Twist and three rates.** Fold and winding sit at the same node. The twist a_J = J/(Mc) is an imaginary displacement of this place. Both multipole series are M·(i·a_J)^l and Q_el·(i·a_J)^l (i the imaginary unit, l the order), hence J = Mc·a_J and μ = Q_el·c·a_J: from this follows, for every mass, charge and rotation, the same ratio of magnetic moment to angular momentum, g = 2. For the electron this is the Bohr magneton μ_B = eħ/(2m_e) = 9.274·10⁻²⁴ J/T. For spin ½, a_J = λ̄/2: the moment is charge times c times half the quantum length, μ = e·c·λ̄/2 = eħ/(2M). For the proton it is μ_N = μ_B·m_e/m_p, the ratio m_p/m_e = 1836.2 is content, not form. Every mass has three rates: the fold rate c/(3Δs), the Compton rate c/λ̄ and the folding rate α_G·c/λ̄. The Compton rate is their geometric mean, once more a mirror. Below the Planck mass the fold rate is the fastest, above it the reverse, at the Planck mass all three are equal. The rate of the inner direction, 1/τ_D = a₀/c, is the same for every mass and meets the Compton rate at M_min, the fold rate at M_U and the folding rate at M_c. The length of the folding rate is the depth mirrored about λ̄, λ̄²/(3Δs), like the hull of the winding a_B = λ̄_e²/r_e.

**Return of the winding.** In the field B the twist turns at the rate eB/M, for g = 2 exactly as fast as the orbit goes round, the orbit as a whole only half as fast. The difference the measurement reads is made by the return of the winding onto itself: counted per radian, with the weight α, once back, μ/μ_B = 1 + α/(2π) = 1.001161, measured 1.001160 (CODATA 2018). Independently, the hydrogen line, winding times twist at the place of the nucleus, reads the first return as 0.88·α/2π: without return 1418.95 MHz, with the first 1420.60 MHz, measured 1420.41 MHz (Hellwig et al. 1970), with g_p = 5.586 as content (CODATA 2018). When the rate eB/M reaches the Compton rate, the field lies at the meeting point of the winding, B = M²c²/(eħ) = 4.41·10⁹ T, E = B·c = 1.32·10¹⁸ V/m (Schwinger field): there pairs arise from the medium.

**Matter in the field.** The three roles give three responses. Tangentially every orbit turns against the field without loss, the diamagnetism of every substance (for the hydrogen atom with ⟨r²⟩ = 3a_B², per mole −μ₀·N_A·e²·a_B²/(2m_e) = −2.99·10⁻¹¹ m³/mol, that is −2.38·10⁻⁶ cm³/mol). Open windings align against the loss kT, paramagnetism, with two senses of circulation in the Fermi form m = tanh(μB/kT), small the Curie law. If the windings of neighbouring atoms lock in the same sense, the field of the others is a feedback with q = T_C/T: the susceptibility is (C/T)·Σqⁿ = C/(T − T_C), the tipping point q = 1 is the Curie temperature, ferromagnetism. Locking in opposite senses is q < 0, the antiferromagnet. Fully locked is the superconductor: two locked windings obey ψ̇ = Δω − K·sin ψ, the equation of the locking of two cycles (Josephson), the phase of the pair closes per circuit with Φ₀ = h/(2e) = 2.068·10⁻¹⁵ Wb, and the transverse channel is closed: no loss, no resistance, the field penetrates only to the closing length λ_L (Meissner).

**Twist of the fold.** Because the fold carries the same multipole series as the winding, a rotating funnel turns along around itself: the plane of an orbit turns with Ω = 2GJ/(c²r³) = 6Δs·c·a_J/r³, the axis of a gyroscope on a polar orbit on average with GJ/(2c²r³) = 3Δs·c·a_J/(2r³), a quarter of that. That is the magnetism of the fold: twist without charge. Conversely the twist of the fold carries no magnetic moment, μ = Q_el·c·a_J is zero without charge. The field of a planet or star is therefore winding, not fold: locked currents (the dynamo as the locking of the flows in the coupling window), no form measure of the rotation. A relation μ ∝ J through √G (Blackett) would mean Q_el/M = √G, the meeting point of charge and mass. The Earth lies far below. In the triad the rotation of the galaxy is its vortex. Its magnetic field is winding of the gas, not twist of the fold, and belongs to the state, not to the form. The magnetism contributes nothing to the restoring of the whole, because ΣQ_el = 0.

## 18 The Nucleus: a Held Funnel

A **held funnel** is a body whose building blocks are held at the distance of a **holding length** λ_H. Its envelope is then R = λ_H·N^{1/3} for N building blocks. The atomic nucleus is such a funnel, held by its nucleons themselves.

**Derivation of the holding length from nuclear masses.** Through the mutual repulsion of its protons, the binding of a nucleus with Z protons and mass number A loses the Coulomb energy a_C·Z(Z − 1)/A^{1/3} with a_C = (3/5)·αħc/λ_H. Fitting the liquid-drop formula to all nuclei of AME2020 gives λ_H = 1.25 fm. Independently of that, **mirror nuclei**, in which Z and N are exchanged, differ only in winding. Their difference in binding is pure Coulomb energy and reads the holder as λ_H = 1.33 fm (36 pairs, AME2020).

The nucleons move inside the nucleus with v/c ≈ λ̄_p/λ_H = 0.17. Half the spread of the binding of ⁶²Ni independently gives 0.14. Size and binding read the same speed up to a factor of order one. Nuclear transitions run by themselves towards the smallest fold per nucleon, at ⁵⁶Fe. The largest binding per nucleon belongs to ⁶²Ni (AME2020).

**The tipping point.** The fissility x = E_C/(2E_S) compares the Coulomb energy E_C of the protons (winding) with the surface energy E_S of the holding. It is the gain of a feedback that tips over at x = 1: the restoring force of a deformation is ∝ 1 − x. The binding per nucleon is largest at x = ¼ (fitted to the measured nuclei of AME2020: x = 0.25). The tipping point is the meeting point of the nucleus. The heaviest known nuclei lie there (Z = 118, A = 294: x = 0.98), while the innermost electron of the same atoms is only at Zα = 0.86: matter ends at the meeting point of the nucleus before the shell reaches its own. Because x grows with α, the coupling of the shell sets the end of the nucleus.

**The limit of the shell.** The heaviest known nuclei are poor in neutrons. The shell holds up to Z = 170: Zα = 1 (Z = 137) holds for a point-like nucleus. Around the nucleus with R = λ_H·A^{1/3} the binding of the innermost electron reaches twice the fold energy of the electron, 6c⁴·Δs_e/G, only there (Dirac equation).

**The shells.** The model's closure numbers give the liquid-drop formula a shell term, and it follows from the funnel itself. The levels of the held funnel with twist (κ = 0.12, μ = 0.03) close at 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82 and bind against their averaged filling with the shell spacing ħω = (5/6)·(3/2)^{4/3}·ħ²/(m_N·λ_H²)·A^{−1/3} = 38.0 MeV·A^{−1/3}. AME2020 reads independently 39.3, hence λ_H = 1.23 fm. Only the binding part counts, for where the shells do not bind, the nucleus deforms. With no free number in the shell term the binding scatters by 1.83 MeV, the deformed nuclei by 1.26 instead of 1.93 MeV.

## 19 The Atom, Closing and Opening

An atom is a small, fast-circulating vortex: tiny fold, large steepness. The shell of hydrogen circulates with v/c = α on the Bohr radius a_B = 0.529·10⁻¹⁰ m, at 6.58·10¹⁵ Hz. The atom too has its three radii: the threshold radius of the proton lies at r* = 32 fm, between nucleus and shell (a_B = 1631·r*). As a compact funnel, its interior remains Newtonian. The binding of the atom comes from the geometry of its shell, not from the depth of its fold.

**Closing forms.** The pure 1/r² slope closes every orbit, and orbits of equal energy bind equally, whatever their shape. This has a reason in form: the momenta of a Kepler orbit lie on a circle, and mapped onto the sphere S³ every orbit is a great circle. In the space of motions, not in position space, the funnel is a closed sphere. The n² states of a shell (n its number) are the vibrations of this sphere, 2n² with both senses of circulation. Only two slopes close every orbit (Bertrand's theorem): the 1/r² slope outside a funnel and the linear one inside a uniformly held body. Their shells close after

```

held body (r¹):         2, 8, 20, 40, 70, 112     O(k) = (k + 1)(k + 2)(k + 3)/3
```

## 20 Matter: the Coupling Window

Two coupled cycles with phase difference ψ obey ψ̇ = Δω − 2k·cos φ·sin ψ, with Δω the difference of their eigenfrequencies, the coupling k (here not the Boltzmann constant) and its phase φ. They lock in when |Δω| ≤ 2k|cos φ|, and their torus then contracts onto a circle. Only the lossy part k·cos φ of the coupling locks, the rest merely shifts the frequencies. Too weak a coupling lets the cycles drift apart. In general, for cycles θ and slow structure Z: θ̇ = ω(Z) + K(θ, Z), Ż = G(θ, Z) − D(Z)·Z, with eigenfrequencies ω, cross-coupling K, feed G and loss D. A common medium with pair coupling k carries at most N ≈ 1 + 2W/(πk) free cycles in a frequency band W. Above that they lock in. If a feedback grows to q = 1, Σqⁿ diverges at this tipping point. Beyond it the hysteresis window opens with the distance from the tipping point to the power 3/2, and before each edge of the window the return rate 1 − q falls with the square root of the distance to the edge (critical slowing down). From T² to T¹ lead three ways: locking (both cycles go on oscillating, bound), kinking, and the loss of a cycle. The number of independent cycles is the number of neutral Lyapunov exponents, transverse to the torus they are negative. Stability is motion without an obligation to repeat: the feedback must take hold before a perturbation builds up. Bifurcations are changes of role: the birth of a cycle makes a transverse direction tangential, locking makes a tangential one transverse. Hysteresis needs no store, the state is only the sequence of the extremes not yet erased. Noise and loss come from the same damping. In between lies the **coupling window** k_min < k < k_lock. A substance is the coupling window of its atoms: solid is locked (the lattice as identification), liquid is inside the window, gaseous is frayed. Melting gives up the identification of the lattice, not the cohesion.

The width of the window depends on the level below: across 93 elements, T_boil/T_melt ranges from 0.81 (As, which sublimes, so the window is closed) to 8.2 (Ga), and the more firmly the atom binds, the narrower the window (rank correlation with the ionisation energy −0.48, PubChem). The trend runs between kinds of bond (noble gases and halogens narrow, metals wide), not within a group.

**Docking.** The same window carries the docking of a drug to its receptor. At the molecule the winding carries, not the fold, and drug and receptor are not things that meet but nodes of the winding that close a common loop. For the drug the body is a closed space whose only outside is excretion. The circulation brings it back with the fraction q = 1 − E per pass, E the fraction that liver and kidney take per pass, and the passes add up to the Bose form of the rule, ν = 1/E. The threshold lies, as at r*, where loss time and circulation time are equal, at E = 1 − 1/e, and the clearance of the whole sets the threshold of every binding site, ℓ_* = √(D/Γ). On every level stands the triad: at the site contact, mouth of the pocket and threshold of return, in the field of receptors bound, near and far, in the body binding site, circulation and excretion.

A site carries at most one partner. With q = [L]/K_d it reads the return with two senses of rotation, θ = q/(1 + q), the Fermi form. A drug that attaches to itself reads the same return with one sense, q/(1 − q), and tips into stacks at q = 1. Rebinding follows the one return equation, and the dimension of the return sets its form: in a channel the residence time grows as 1/√Γ, on a surface only logarithmically, in free solution it stays finite. Uptake and release are mirror images in time, so the field of receptors stretches both rates equally. It sets the clock of binding, not its depth K_d. ```

Not the phase enters the clock but the folding of its field, and that depends on no mass: like ℓ_P, √(r_Q·3Δs) lies at the middle of the edge between tangential and parametric, times the charge in q_P. The horizons are the roots of r² − 6Δs·r + a_J² + r_Q·3Δs = 0. Their sum remains the depth, their product is carried by twist and winding: the height in the Thales circle becomes the hypotenuse of a_J and √(r_Q·3Δs), and both horizons meet at j² + r_Q/(3Δs) = 1. Twist and winding count against the same coupling, j = (J/ħ)/α_G and r_Q/(3Δs) = (Q_el/q_P)²/α_G. Without rotation the meeting point lies at Q_el/q_P = M/m_Pl, the meeting point of charge and mass. With r_e/(3Δs_e) = α/α_G(m_e) = 4.2·10⁴² the electron lies far above it in the winding as well.

Without rotation M_irr is the folding within the horizon, M = M_irr + Q_el²/(8πε₀c²r₊), and only the field outside can be extracted. With rotation M² = (M_irr + M_el)² + M_rot² with M_el = Q_el²/(16πε₀G·M_irr): the field of the winding adds to M_irr linearly as folding to folding, the twist stands across both and counts in the square. As M_irr·M_rot depends only on the rotation, M_irr·M_el = (m_Pl·Q_el/(2q_P))² depends only on the charge, in lengths r₀(M_irr)·r₀(M_el) = r_Q·3Δs. Without rotation and at fixed charge the mass is smallest where both are equal, M_irr = M_el = M/2, at the meeting point r_Q = 3Δs. There half of the rest energy can be extracted, and the entropy is a quarter of that of the uncharged fold of equal mass. In general the temperature reads the distance of the horizons against the area, ħc·(r₊ − r₋)/(4πk·r₀(M_irr)²), without winding with r₀(M_irr)² = r₀·r₊.

**Loss per turn.** Every orbit runs tangentially. Left open, it gives off a part into the transversal direction per turn, and how much depends on the number of senses of circulation. The winding has two senses, the centre of its charge can oscillate, it emits as a dipole. The fold has one sense, its centre stands still, it emits only as a quadrupole. Per turn, against the binding of the orbit:

```
winding:  ΔE/E = (8π/3)·(r_e/r)·(v/c)        hydrogen: (8π/3)·α³ = 3.3·10⁻⁶
fold:     ΔE/E = (128π/5)·(m/M)·(v/c)⁵       Earth around the Sun: 2.3·10⁻²⁴
```

Left open, the winding of hydrogen would fall onto the nucleus in 1.6·10⁻¹¹ s. It does not fall, because its wave closes after one turn: the closure keeps the transversal direction shut, emission happens only at the change between closed forms. The same number of senses of circulation that lets windings cancel and folds add lets the winding emit as a dipole and the fold only as a quadrupole.

**Fall and braking.** The lowest order of emission, l = 1 for the winding and l = 2 for the fold, also sets how fast the emission grows with the motion. On an orbit the power is P ∝ r^{−(l+3)} at the binding E ∝ 1/r. Until the fall passes t = E/((l + 2)·P): left open, the winding falls after a third, the fold after a quarter of the time its initial loss per turn would need for the whole binding. The Earth would so fall onto the Sun in 1.1·10²³ years. Two equal masses with δ = 3Δs/a, Δs that of one, merge after t = (5/512)·(3Δs/c)·δ⁻⁴ in N = δ^{−5/2}/(64√2·π) turns. The number of turns depends on the spread alone, the time counts in the light time of the depth: from δ = 0.001 on 1.1·10⁵ turns, for one solar mass each in 13 hours. A rotating body emits with P ∝ Ω^{2l+2} and brakes with Ω̇ ∝ Ωⁿ, braking index n = 2l + 1: n = 3 through the winding of locked currents whose axis stands oblique to the axis of rotation, n = 5 through a deformation of its fold across the axis. As a dipole the twist of the fold emits nothing, for it is the rotation itself, J = Mc·a_J, and stands fixed like its centre. Orbit and rotation are one form: if the emission grows with the energy as P ∝ E^k, the end or the start lies t = E/((k − 1)·P) away. The orbit gains binding, k = l + 3, and falls in finite time. The rotation loses energy, k = l + 1, never stands still and has turned with Ω for at most t = Ω/((n − 1)·|Ω̇|) = E/(l·P), through the winding E/P, through the fold E/(2P).

**Mirror of emission.** With |k − 1|, t = E/(|k − 1|·P) also holds for k below one and for an energy that grows. Over ln E, ln P is a straight line of slope k. Slope 1 keeps the time E/P fixed, and t is E/P divided by the distance of the slope from 1. The fold radiates with P ∝ M⁻², k = −2, and lives E/(3P). At the limit it grows with k = 1 by the factor e per time E/P, without end and without start in finite time. The mirror M ↔ m̃ turns P ∝ E^k into the rate of Ẽ = m̃c², (Ẽ/E)·P ∝ Ẽ^{2 − k}. It mirrors the slope about 1, reverses k − 1 and keeps the time fixed. Only growth at the limit stays fixed: if the fold grows there, its mirror mass decreases by the factor e in the same time. When the fold evaporates, k = −2 becomes mirrored 2 − k = 4: its temperature rises with Ṫ ∝ T⁴ and reaches the end after the same time E/(3P).

**Fall in every field.** On a circular orbit in a field of flux dimension d, g ∝ r^{1 − d}, v² ∝ r^{2 − d}, and per e-fold of the radius the binding changes by (4 − d)·E_kin, with the same 4 − d as in κ² = (4 − d)·Ω². The emission of order l grows as r^{2l}·Ω^{2l + 2}, and until the fall passes

```
t = (4 − d)·E_kin/((l·(d − 2) + 2)·P)
```

For Newton, d = 3, that is E/((l + 2)·P). In the deep regime, d = 2, the speed is fixed: every order emits with P ∝ v^{2l + 2}/r², the binding counts in e-folds of the radius, per factor m·v_flat², and every orbit falls in t = E_kin/P, whether it emits through the winding or the fold. Over ln E, ln P is no longer a straight line there, over E itself it is one, and r² decreases uniformly with time. At d = 4 the binding does not depend on the radius, and every loss is the fall.

## 22 Galaxies: the Rule in the Data

A galaxy is a collective funnel: stars are **single funnels**, while the stellar system as a whole follows the rule in its total field. Compact funnels, denser than the tidal density of their surroundings, remain Newtonian up to their smoothing length. Only the collective field, smoothed over them, is enhanced. The tidal density is ρ_J = 3𝒯/(4πG) with the tide 𝒯 of the smoothed parent field, and the smoothing length ℓ follows from ℓ³ = GM/𝒯. Because the rule acts at the place on the smoothed field, in which the body's own field vanishes at its centre of mass, bodies at the same place fall alike. Compact globular clusters (g = 25·a₀) show hardly any addition (ν = 1.007), thin systems (g = 0.13·a₀) the full one (ν = 3.3). Pressure-supported spheres have, isotropically and for every profile, σ_v⁴ = (4/81)·GMa₀ (σ_v the speed of their thermal motion): 9.1 km/s at 10⁷ M☉, 19.3 km/s at 2·10⁸ M☉. For an isotropic swarm of test bodies with ρ ∝ r^{−γ} in the deep regime of a source, the Jeans equation gives σ² = v_flat²/γ at every radius: without pressure from outside every such swarm is isothermal there, its dispersion reads the source only through v_flat and itself only through its profile. Its mass outwards is finite only for γ > 3, the dimension of space: gas that does not carry the field itself has a finite mass only with σ < v_flat/√3 = 0.58·v_flat. The isothermal halo the observer reads, ρ_X ∝ r^{−2}, is the swarm with γ = 2, its mass grows without end. If an isothermal sphere entirely in the deep regime carries itself, σ⁴ = v_flat⁴/γ² = (4/81)·GMa₀, so γ = 9/2: it falls off outside like r^{−9/2}, virial theorem and profile read the same number. The rule lies everywhere above its deep limit, ν > 1/s: therefore 9/2 is the upper bound, with a Newtonian core the sphere falls off less steeply. Around a region that follows the rule as a whole, the Bondi radius GM/c_s² lies where the spread of the funnel reaches that of the gas, δ = (c_s/c)², and the speed after Newton is c_s. Its mirror image about r* is c_s²/a₀ = ℓ₀·(c_s/c)², where the speed of the threshold is c_s, as ℓ₀ is the mirror image of 3Δs at c_s = c. If c_s < v_flat, it moves beyond r* into the deep regime, and there it does not exist: the orbital speed of the rule never falls below v_flat, acceleration of the orbit and pressure force per mass both fall like 1/r, the scale-invariant deep regime has no radius. Only v_flat/c_s decides: isothermal gas is there the swarm with γ = (v_flat/c_s)². As a whole, a system lies below the threshold with surface density Σ < a₀/(πG) = 242 M☉/pc².

**Surroundings and dimension.** The rule acts on the total field of a region's own masses, not particle by particle. If the region lies in the field of a larger one, it falls with it: the uniform field cancels in the fall, and inside, the region reads only its own folding. What stays effective is the tide, and it decides whether the region remains collective. As a whole it moves in the enhanced field of its host. At the same time the rule lowers the dimension of every form: Newton is flux through the spherical surface, the deep regime flux through a circumference, d_eff = 1 + (d − 1)(1 − χ/2). The sphere goes from 3 to 2, a filament from 2 to 1.5, a wall stays at 1. A filament of line mass μ has the threshold r*_fil = 2Gμ/a₀ (at 10¹² M☉/Mpc: 2.6 kpc). Deep inside it the circular speed grows like r^{1/4}. The epicycles change as well: instead of κ² + ν_z² = 2Ω² (ν_z the frequency of the oscillation perpendicular to the disc), (2 + χ)Ω² holds. The observer who assumes unchanged rules reads the missing part as a density, 4πGρ_X = χΩ², outside an isothermal halo.

**Surroundings in the data.** The data carry this. Around isolated galaxies the lensing follows the rule without external field (KiDS-1000, Brouwer 2021): for the blue ones, which still form stars, χ² = 21 in 15 bins, with 0.003·a₀ in magnitude already 534. The 22 classical satellites of the Local Group, whose host field exceeds their own, lie +0.15 ± 0.04 dex above the rule without the host field, with it in magnitude +0.46 ± 0.05.

**In the data (SPARC).** On the rotation curves of 141 galaxies with 2688 points, with distance, inclination and mass-to-light ratio estimated for each galaxy, the threshold reads a₀ = 1.11·10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (16 to 84 %: 1.00 to 1.21). The mass-to-light ratio of the discs follows from the requirement that stars and gas obey the same rule, because the rule acts on the fold, not on the state of matter: Υ_d = 0.67. With the value of the stellar models, Υ_d = 0.5, one would read a higher threshold and an apparent trend from stars to gas, two consequences of the same foreign conversion. The central surface density from the rotation curve lies, galaxy by galaxy with its own baryons, on average 0.05 dex below the rule, compatible. The Bose form fits best. The simple form, ν = ½ + √(¼ + a₀/a_N), and the standard form, ν = √(½ + √(¼ + (a₀/a_N)²)), are worse by Δχ² = 53 and 41 (χ² the error measure of the fit, not the switch χ). The return has dimension 0.92 ± 0.05, one-dimensional as the square root in q requires. More precisely it is the first passage of a random walk in one dimension: the time T in which it covers the distance of the return is distributed as T^{−3/2}, and its mean ⟨e^{−x·T}⟩ is e^{−√x} with x = a_N/a₀, equal to 10⁻¹⁵ when integrated over the distribution. In three dimensions a random walk would return only with 0.341, the sum of returns would stay at 1.516, and there would be no flat curves. The residual per galaxy depends neither on mass nor on gas fraction, neither on shape (Hubble type) nor on surface brightness: the rule reads only the fold. Stacked by the fold of the baryons, at most 0.02 dex of the rule remains in every half decade.

**Against the halo.** For the same curves the standard model needs an NFW halo with two free numbers per galaxy. The rule has χ² = 2918, the halos 1434 with 282 more free numbers. By the information measure BIC (χ² plus the number of free numbers times ln 2688) the rule leads by 736, 2926 against 3661. For 31 of the 141 galaxies the halos lie at the edge of their range, and their mass scatters against that of the baryons by 0.58 dex: the halo is bookkeeping, not an object of its own.

**Discs at the threshold.** The disc scale length R_d is, in the median, 0.71·r* (16 to 84 %: 0.37 to 1.28) across four decades of mass. That is r*/√2: the centre of the disc lies exactly at the threshold of surface density, Σ₀ = M/(2πR_d²) = a₀/(πG). It grows with the baryonic mass as M_b^{0.31 ± 0.02}: the envelope as at fixed density of the whole with M^{1/3}, the threshold from the galaxy's own fold with M^{1/2}, hence R/r* ∝ M^{−1/6}, like R_ta/r* in the inactive measure (Section 26).

**Response to a perturbation.** From a = ν(|g_N|)·g_N at the place of the orbit a perturbation gives

```
δa = ν·[δg_N − (χ/2)·ĝ(ĝ·δg_N)]
```

Along the slope the rule answers with its derivative, da/da_N = ν(1 − χ/2), across it with its enhancement ν, deep along it with ν/2, because in the mean √(a_N·a₀) the perturbation moves only the slope. A radial wave in the midplane of a thin disc lies along the slope and rearranges the bound baryons themselves. So the return answers as well, and locally and axisymmetrically Toomre's criterion carries G·ν(1 − χ/2) instead of G:

```
ω² = κ² − 2πG·ν(1 − χ/2)·Σ·|k| + σ²k²,     Q_T = κσ/(πG·ν(1 − χ/2)·Σ),     κ² = R·dΩ²/dR + 4Ω²,  Ω² = a/R
```

For stars 2π·max_x (1 − e^{−x}I₀(x))/√x = 3.36 replaces π. At the threshold of surface density the mass drops out, a_N/a₀ = (R/R_d)·[I₀K₀ − I₁K₁](R/(2R_d)) (Freeman). The dispersion at the limit Q_T = 1 is therefore the same function of R/R_d for every mass. In units of the orbital speed v at the same place it is, for a disc of gas, 0.259·v at R_d and 0.144·v at 2R_d (there ν = 2.16, along the slope ν(1 − χ/2) = 1.382). It falls outward: if the gas disperses with more than 0.224·v_flat, every disc at the threshold stays stable outside R_d. At 6·10¹⁰ M☉ the gas needs at least 26.7 km/s at 2R_d for this. Perpendicular to the disc, in its midplane, the rule answers across, with ν like the orbit: ν_z/Ω is the Newtonian ratio. The observer with a halo reads the same curve with a rigid mass that does not answer the wave. The observer computes 0.104·v (19.3 km/s) at 2R_d and reads Q_T too large by the factor ν(1 − χ/2). From the oscillation perpendicular to the thin disc the observer reads ν times its density in the midplane, from its stability ν(1 − χ/2) times. Read as matter, the disc would have two densities, their ratio is 1 − χ/2 = d ln a/d ln a_N. This is a prediction for the local dispersions.

**Weight of a layer.** In a region whose mode has been handed over, the Newtonian field perpendicular to a thin layer of surface density Σ grows with the mass between −z and z, u = 4πG·∫₀^z ρ dz′, up to 2πGΣ at its edge. The rule reads the magnitude together with the field g_R along the layer, a_z = ν(√(g_R² + u²))·u. The midplane carries the weight of the layer, for every profile and every dispersion:

```
P₀ = ∫ρ·a_z dz = (a₀²/2πG)·∫_{s_R}^{s_o} x³·ν(x) dx,     s_R² = g_R/a₀,     s_o⁴ = s_R⁴ + (2πGΣ/a₀)²
```

The column of the layer runs through the ladder from s_R in the midplane to s_o at the edge. The spontaneous part x³ is Spitzer's layer, P₀ = πGΣ²/2, isothermal ρ = ρ₀·sech²(z/z₀) with z₀ = σ²/(πGΣ). The return adds x³·n_B, the stage d = 3 of the ladder, the structure action. If the layer is thin against the field along it, 2πGΣ ≪ g_R, it is Spitzer's layer with G·ν(g_R), the answer across the slope, z₀ = σ²/(πG·ν·Σ). In a disc at the threshold the midplane at 2R_d carries 2.08 times Spitzer's weight, just below ν. Without a field along the layer the field vanishes in the middle of every layer, and there it is deep. Below the threshold, 2πGΣ ≪ a₀, P₀ = (Σ/3)·√(2πGΣa₀) = (2/3)·√((πGΣ²/2)·Σa₀), the mean of Spitzer's weight and that of the threshold, Σ·a₀, as the orbit in the deep regime is the mean of slope and threshold. Isothermally half of the mass then lies within 0.888·σ²/√(2πGΣa₀) instead of 0.549·σ²/(πGΣ). Far above it the column runs through the whole stage, P₀ = πGΣ²/2 + π³a₀²/(30G). The return gives no more to the midplane of any layer: at most π³a₀²/(30G) = ρ_s·c² = 1.74·10⁻¹⁰ Pa, independent of the mass. The same constant π⁴/15 gives, over all s of the whole, the restoring of one direction, the vacuum part, and over the column of a layer the largest weight of the return. A layer of gas that never cooled reads (1 + Q)·Newton, and its addition Q·πGΣ²/2 has no bound. This is a prediction.

**Friction.** A body of mass m that moves with v through the bound baryons of a region whose mode has been handed over (density ρ_b, dispersion σ) gathers a wake behind it that brakes it (Chandrasekhar). Raising the wake and being pulled by it are both responses to a disturbance: a wave k of the wake is raised with G·ν[1 − (χ/2)(k̂·ĝ)²], and across the slope it pulls with G·ν. Up to its smoothing length ℓ, ℓ³ = Gm/𝒯 with the tide 𝒯 = −dg/dr, a compact body is a single funnel, and there Newton counts for both. On a circular orbit the path lies across the slope, and for an isotropic dispersion

```
dv/dt = −(4πG²·m·ρ_b/v²)·F(X)·[ln(ℓ/b_min) + ν²·(1 − (χ/4)·(1 − μ̄²))·ln(b_max/ℓ)],     F(X) = erf X − (2X/√π)·e^{−X²},  X = v/(√2·σ)
```

F(X) is the share of stars slower than the body, μ̄² the mean square of the cosine μ between wave and path, weighted like the braking with μ²·e^{−X²μ²}: 0 for fast bodies and 3/5 for slow ones. b_max is the distance from the centre, b_min the larger of the radius of the body and Gm/v², where its spread reaches that of the motion, 3Δs/b = (v/c)², as at the Bondi radius. In the Newtonian range this is Chandrasekhar with the baryons alone. The slope reads the rule once, friction reads it twice, ν² = Σ(n + 1)·qⁿ: n returns split in n + 1 ways between raising and pulling. Beyond ℓ the rule brakes a fast body at the threshold 2.14 times, at a_N = 0.1·a₀ 10.7 times as strongly as Newton with the same baryons. Read as matter, friction would have a density of its own, ν²·(1 − (χ/4)·(1 − μ̄²))·ρ_b, quadratic in the response. The observer with a halo computes friction at the same dispersion with the density read from the slope, in a sphere ν(1 − χ/2)·ρ_b + χΩ²/(4πG). The two part outside the baryons: there no wake forms, while the observer computes friction with χΩ²/(4πG). This is a prediction.

**Relaxation.** In a collective region of N stars of mass m, with M = N·m and radius R, whose mode has been handed over, every encounter at distance b gives a star moving with speed v the kick δv = 2Gm/(bv). Up to its smoothing length ℓ the kicking star is a single funnel and kicks after Newton. Beyond it, its field is a disturbance of the smoothed field, and the rule answers along the slope with ν(1 − χ/2), across it with ν. In the spherical mean, one direction along and two across, the square of the kick grows by the factor ν²·f with f = (2 + (1 − χ/2)²)/3, deep ¾. In the time R/v

```
Δv²/v² = (8/N)·(GM/(v²R))²·[ln(ℓ/b_min) + ν²·f·ln(R/ℓ)],     b_min = Gm/v²,     ℓ³ = Gm/𝒯
```

b_min is the distance at which the spread of the star reaches that of the motion, as for friction. In the deep regime the tide at the edge is 𝒯 = Ω² = v²/R², so ℓ³ = b_min·R²: the smoothing length divides ln(R/b_min) at one third, two thirds near, one third far. Without memory v² = ν·GM/R. The field of a far star and the square of the speed then both grow by the factor ν: in Δv²/v² the far part stays as after Newton up to f, the near part falls by 1/ν². In the time R/v there remains (8/N)·ln(R/b_min)·(2/(3ν²) + f/3), deep 2·ln(R/b_min)/N, a quarter of the Newtonian value at equal logarithm. The observer with a halo computes at the same speed only with the kicks of the stars after Newton, (8/N)·(GM/(v²R))²·ln(R/b_min). The region thus relaxes (2 + ν²·f)/3 times as fast as this observer expects, with ν and χ of the return, which reads the baryons once bound: at a_N = 0.13·a₀ (ν = 3.3) 3.5 times, at a_N = 0.01·a₀ (ν = 10.5) 29 times. Read as matter, the halo would be grainy: without memory it would consist of bodies of (ν²·f − 1)/(3(ν − 1)) stellar masses, there 1.1 and 2.9. This is a prediction for the relaxation times.

**Arms.** A wave in the disc turns with the shear from leading to trailing and is amplified most when its crests stand radially. Then k lies azimuthally, across the slope, and the wave arises with G·ν. Its critical length there is λ_T = 4π²G·ν·Σ/κ², and around the circumference fit

```
m_T = 2πR/λ_T = κ²R/(2πG·ν·Σ) = (4 − d_eff)·g_N/(2πGΣ),     −d ln Ω/d ln R = d_eff/2
```

Orbit and wave both read the full enhancement ν, and it cancels. So m_T reads only the baryons, the ratio of their field in the plane to their field perpendicular at the disc, and through the flux dimension the shear. The number of arms is m_T/X with the measure X of the swing amplification. At the threshold of surface density m_T is the same function of R/R_d for every mass, 2.16 at R_d, 3.45 at 2R_d (there d_eff = 1.58). Newton with the baryons alone would give 2.98 there: only the shear differs. The observer with a halo computes with a rigid mass that does not answer the wave and expects, at the same X, ν times as many, 7.45 at 2R_d. From the number of arms the observer reads, as from the oscillation perpendicular to the disc, ν times the surface density. This is a prediction.

**Memory of binding.** The return of a bound region reads the baryons it has bound, the slope at the place only those in it today: a = a_N + (ν(a_N,B) − 1)·a_N,B, with a_N,B from the fold of those once bound. This is the decomposition of the rule after Einstein, ν = 1 + n_B: Newton, the spontaneous part, reads at the place what is there today, the return n_B, the stimulated part, in the inner direction what was bound. Discs that still form stars still carry their gas: in SPARC the residual depends neither on the gas fraction nor on the type.

**Tidal dwarfs.** In an encounter the tide tears gas out of the disc of a galaxy whose mode has been handed over, and in the tail it gathers into dwarfs. Their gas has cooled and is bound: such a dwarf no longer breathes with the whole and, by the state of its source, reads the rule, not (1 + Q)·Newton. As a system of gas and stars it is collective and a region of its own out to its Jacobi radius, and also close to the host it reads only its own fold. It has bound only what is in it today: the memory adds nothing, and young and gas-rich it hardly depends on the mass-to-light ratio. It therefore lies on the rule with its present baryons like an isolated gas-rich dwarf, not above it like the classical satellites. The host keeps reading the gas that went into the tail in its return, the dwarf in its own: two returns read the same gas, for the return is rule, not matter. A dwarf of 10⁸ M☉ of gas has r* = 0.36 kpc and, on a circular orbit at a distance of 50 kpc from the Milky Way, binds out to 6.8 kpc. At 2 kpc it rotates by the rule with 36.0 km/s, ν = 6.03. The observer with a halo expects Newton there, 14.7 km/s. The halo of that observer around the Milky Way is the swarm with γ = 2 and σ = v_flat/√2 = 120.5 km/s, and by Newton the dwarf holds at 2 kpc only what moves slower than 20.7 km/s. Even fully captured, that halo at the place, 4πGρ_X = χΩ², would carry only 7.2·10⁶ M☉ within 2 kpc. Reading the rule in the dwarf as matter, the observer needs 5.0·10⁸ M☉ of dark matter in it, which cannot get there. This is a prediction for the tidal dwarfs.

**Galaxies in clusters.** A galaxy whose mode has been handed over reads its own rule inside a cluster, while the tide of the cluster reads the state of its source, (1 + Q)·Newton around the gas that never cooled, 𝒯 = d_eff·Ω² with the orbital rate Ω in the cluster. Its edge is the Jacobi radius, where its field reaches the tide, deep at the edge r_J = v_flat/(√d_eff·Ω): the same form as R_max = v_flat/H_∞ on its own, with the orbital rate of the cluster instead of the rate of the end state. Against its threshold r_J/r* = a₀/(√d_eff·Ω·v_flat). It keeps its deep regime as long as the product of its speed and the orbital rate of the cluster stays below the threshold. The Milky Way (6·10¹⁰ M☉) in a cluster of (1 + Q)·M_b = 10¹⁵ M☉, taken as a point mass with d_eff = 3, thus reaches at a distance of 1 Mpc out to 50 kpc instead of 3.1 Mpc and loses its deep regime within 0.33 Mpc. This is a prediction.

**What the observer sees.** Whoever assumes unchanged rules converts the addition into a mass:

```
M_apparent/M = 1/(1 − e^{−s}) = r/r* + ½ + r*/(12r) − (r*/r)³/720 + …
```

The fixed half ½ and the coefficients 1/12 and 1/720 are exactly the local coefficients of the ladder of steps. They are Bernoulli numbers: the error of the single returns against the continuum, as of the polygon against the circle. The additional potential that the observer ascribes to an invisible mass is Φ_X = v_flat²·ln ν(s), the logarithm of the enhancement. The density that is read off is an output of the observer's calculation, not a content of space. ## 24 The Closure Rule and Q

In the background radiation and in the build-up of clusters, the local rule is not enough. There a restriction of the degrees of freedom of the whole acts, the **closure rule**, not a substance:

```
(∂ξ)² = −1          the pressure direction of the conformal mode is closed
ż = (I − w·Π⊥)F      Π⊥ projects onto the escape direction (pressure, scattering)
w = 1 − e^{−√(θ̃τ_D)}   weight: 1 co-breathing, 0 bound
```

F is the vector field of the motion, I the identity. The **mode** ξ is an oscillation form of the whole whose pressure direction is closed. The **weight** w says how strongly it acts, and it depends on the expansion rate θ̃ of the region: if the region breathes along, w ≈ 1. If it is bound, w goes to zero. As long as a region breathes along (w ≈ 1), the mode is read like pressureless dust with the weight

```
Q = q/(1 − q) = Ω_c/Ω_b
```

With the measured Q = 5.36, q = 0.843. By Kac, 1 − q = Ω_b/Ω_m is the measure of the open region and 1 + Q the mean return time of the whole. What cosmology reads as dark matter Ω_c is this mode. Linearly this is identical with the standard model. The mode has no initial data of its own, it starts in the state of the baryons. From the turnaround point of a region onward, it passes into the local rule, about one radian of the orbit after the turnaround. The balance of whole and place closes at the **seam** R_Q = r*/(−ln q), where the local q reaches the cosmic one (a_N = 0.029·a₀, Milky Way R_Q = 52.0 kpc). There χ = Q·(−ln q) = 0.917, almost the deep limit, inside the local rule acts, outside 1 + Q counts as the measure of the whole. In SPARC 131 points lie beyond the seam, their residual against the local rule is +0.013 ± 0.006 dex, capped at 1 + Q they would lie 0.094 dex above. Likewise the lensing around isolated galaxies follows the local rule up to 2.6 Mpc. In clusters the mode is not handed over: their acceleration follows the measure of the whole, (1 + Q)·g_bar, down into the core, not the local rule. It is the same formula with the return number at the nome of the whole, n_B(q) = q/(1 − q) = Q, instead of at the nome of the place, and at the seam both are equal. The mode passes over only where the phase-space sheet folds, and gas folds only once it has given off its heat: cold gas breaks up into clouds whose orbits cross, hot plasma holds itself up by its pressure, and the sheet does not fold. The plasma of clusters has never given off its heat, in the core its cooling takes 13.6 to 38 Gyr (X-COP). **Sunyaev-Zeldovich.** Light of the background radiation that runs through the hot plasma of a cluster scatters off electrons with the spread per direction δ_e = kT_e/(m_e c²).
